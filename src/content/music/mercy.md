Like a lot of flesh releases, this was heavily inspired by a beloved book. Matt had gotten real into [Death In Spring](https://www.openletterbooks.org/products/death-in-spring) by Mercè Rodoreda, [wonderfully translated by Martha Tennent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ck3R_iZZJx4).^1 He encouraged me to check it out and the *I* got hooked haha. That book has some of the most incredible, surreal prose. It's simultaneously very moving and upsetting, which drove a lot of what we wanted to do with this.

Matt deserves credit for the big sweeping structure of this album, the title track especially. We hashed out a lot of the big riffs and bits and bobs across the record, but when it comes to the form of the thing, it would be absurd to call this anything but Matt's concept. I spent something like two years working on Rites, churned out Wisteria in an afternoon, and helped Matt develop some of the other riffs and arrangement, but he really pulled out the stops structuring and mapping this out and I don't know that there wasn't a day for nearly 3 years where he wasn't working on getting Mercy (the title track) as good as it could be.

These songs were enormously complex with so many one-offs and required a lot of attention to detail. I can distinctly remember leaving Matt's house deeply frustrated at my own inability to remember it all. The songs as written pushed me REALLY hard, and I just couldn't get my fingers to move the way I wanted them to. When this one was done and out, I felt like a very good guitarist, but I really decided to take a step back.

## Rites

Rites was my baby for this record. I spent something like two years picking and prodding at that track. We were all DEEPLY serious about critiques and no one pulled any punches, which was a bit anxiety inducing, but it's very hard to argue with the results. I'm definitely more happy with this than the initial demos. 

Big reference point for me on this was [Warning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=42S17rDYZuc&list=RD42S17rDYZuc&start_radio=1) and a little three note inversion guitar trick they use. I also took an idea from ["You Never Give Me Your Money"](https://www.icce.rug.nl/~soundscapes/DATABASES/AWP/yngmym.shtml) (see section Z for the direct inspiration)for the breakdown section of Rites that uses this very cool minor third up key modulation (until you hit your home key again an octave up.) Was real psyched about the harmonies and whatnot on this record (mad cred to Rachel who I dragged into doing this and still proceeded to really commit). The flourishes were all incredibly breathtaking once they were properly hashed out.

## Negative Harmony Trick

Quite proud of a specific harmonic trick that Matt and I worked out on Mercy where we flip the 3rd interval harmony and melody on a guitar riff so that the harmony sits in the octave below the melody (becoming a 6th interval). Sometimes less conventional "theory" can scream "look at me! look at how smart I am!!!", but this felt like a great practical application of the concept. We brought it in for a variation on one of the riffs and it totally shifted the perception of the theme in a way that felt right at home with the nature of the song.
